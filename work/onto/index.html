Selected work · ONTO

Less searching.
More getting out.

ONTO brings events and places into one location-based PWA. The goal is simple: help someone find something nearby worth doing, then make it easy to act on it.

01 · The problem

The evening is short.
The search shouldn't be.

A concert has a ticketing page. A library has a calendar. A community group has its own listing. Finding something to do can mean checking all of them, then working out whether the time, distance, and cost fit.

I built ONTO to bring that search together. It includes nearby events and destinations such as parks, trails, and indoor spaces. Browsing works without an account; someone who wants synchronized plans can choose to sign in.

I work across the product and its implementation, using AI-assisted development alongside source review and verification. That includes deciding what the interface asks of a person and how incoming event data becomes a useful feed.

02 · The experience

A place, a choice,
and a next step.

  1. Choose where to look. Use a supported city, device location, or a temporary map pin. Events and Places share that choice.
  2. Narrow the possibilities. Search events, change distance or sorting, and inspect the event's details. Places can take time of day and weather into account.
  3. Act on the result. Follow the original source, get directions, or save a private Going plan. Guest plans work without signup; accounts add synchronization.

The interesting engineering work is often in the less convenient version of that flow: location is denied, a calendar is incomplete, a saved event has changed, or the connection has gone away.

03 · Engineering decisions

A simple feed needs
careful boundaries.

One location context across the app

A selected city and a device fix are different kinds of information. ONTO resolves them into one browsing context, including the service region and timezone. Both discovery views use it, so changing location does not leave Places looking somewhere else.

The tradeoff: when a location cannot be resolved, discovery has to explain what it needs instead of quietly treating an old coordinate as a new GPS fix.

Normalize sources before they reach the screen

Ticketing feeds and publisher calendars arrive in different formats. Registered readers feed a shared pipeline for validation, attribution, deduplication, and regional checks. Source names and ID prefixes establish ownership, helping keep one reader from overwriting another source's events.

The tradeoff: adding a publisher takes more work than collecting its URL. The reader has to preserve identity, dates, and provenance, including when a refresh is only partly successful.

Keep feed decisions testable

Feed filtering and ranking live in a pure TypeScript core. Time and location arrive as inputs. The interface wires those decisions to state and persistence, rather than hiding the rules inside a screen.

The tradeoff: the boundaries need explicit contracts, but the decisions can be exercised without a live device, account, or database.

Give offline recovery an honest limit

Cached public events and bundled regional snapshots provide fallback discovery. They cannot make an old listing fresh. Current events, weather, and account synchronization still depend on connectivity.

04 · Privacy

Make each disclosure
a deliberate choice.

Browsing does not require a login. Precise device and pin coordinates are kept in memory; catalog requests identify a service region. Local preferences and cached data support the experience, while optional accounts introduce account and plan records.

That makes privacy an engineering constraint, not a claim that the app handles no data. Maps and weather also have their own network boundaries. Those distinctions matter when explaining what stays on the device and what another service receives.

05 · Where it stands

A working product.
An ongoing discipline.

ONTO is in active PWA development. The codebase brings discovery, shared location controls, source ingestion, guest plans, and optional account synchronization into one product. Feature availability depends on the deployed build and its configuration.

The next work is the work that makes an aggregator useful over time: reliable event coverage, understandable empty states, and verified mobile installation, offline recovery, and updates.

What this project demonstrates

Taking a practical problem through product choices, interface work, data contracts, privacy boundaries, and delivery—and being able to explain the compromises along the way.
